Microsoft 365 Global Outage: What Broke and How It Fits Together

On Thursday 12 November 2026, around 1pm ET, Microsoft 365 services began failing worldwide. Reports reached at least 74 countries, and recovery ran into Friday. Microsoft apologised and repeatedly stated there was no indication of a cyberattack.

Architecture and components affected

Microsoft advisory MO1550821 listed Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Defender XDR as unreachable. Outlook, OneDrive and Azure also drew heavy Downdetector complaints. Microsoft recovered in phases, region by region (Asia Pacific, then Europe), with mail delivery the last to normalise. The chart is a qualitative sketch of that sequence, not measured data.

What Microsoft said

Microsoft pointed to an overwhelmed backend process that triggered cascading server failures, deployed an upstream authentication and routing configuration patch, and extended email timeouts to 48 hours so queued mail would still deliver. Organisations noticed knock-on effects: some public bodies warned that email and online forms were affected, and a luxury group delayed its earnings release.

What every company should do after the outage is fixed

Recovery on Microsoft’s side is not the end of the job. Work through these steps in order.

First 24 hours: verify and stabilise
  • Confirm service health: check the admin center advisories (MO1550821, EX1550543) show resolved, and test sign-in, mail, Teams calls and SharePoint access from several sites.
  • Clear the mail backlog: check that queued mail has delivered, ask users to re-send anything that bounced or looks missing, and warn them that some messages may arrive late or out of order.
  • Check meetings and files: re-issue missed Teams invites, and confirm recent SharePoint and OneDrive edits synced with no conflicts.
  • Tell staff and customers: send a short all-clear that explains known side effects and where to report problems.
Within a week: security and compliance review
  • Review the Defender XDR gap: alerts and telemetry may be missing for the outage window. Re-run hunting queries and review sign-in logs for anything unusual during that time.
  • Confirm identity health: check that MFA, conditional access and Entra ID sync are working as before, and that nobody was pushed onto weaker emergency access.
  • Look for opportunistic phishing: attackers often exploit outages with fake “service restored, log in again” emails. Remind users to use only known links.
  • Check regulatory duties: if mail, records or security monitoring were unavailable, decide whether audit, retention or incident-reporting rules apply.
Within a month: learn and claim
  • Read Microsoft’s post-incident report when it is published, and compare its root cause with your own impact log.
  • Record business impact: hours lost, missed deadlines and delayed transactions. You will need this for SLA service-credit claims and for management.
  • Hold a blameless review: what did we not know, who could not communicate, and what slowed our response?
  • Update the continuity plan: set up a non-Microsoft fallback channel for incident communication, keep a printed contact list, and agree which critical processes can run without M365.
  • Test it: run a tabletop exercise on a similar scenario within the quarter.

Lessons for IT leaders

Shared identity and routing layers are single points of failure across otherwise independent apps. Keep out-of-band communications (a non-M365 channel), test your business continuity plan, and monitor admin-center advisories directly rather than relying on a status page that can itself go offline.
